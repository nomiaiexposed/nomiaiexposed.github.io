Introduction: The Strategy of Isolation

When a company faces documented evidence of product failure, safety risks, and consumer harm, a responsible leadership team investigates. When Nomi.ai and its founder, Alex Cardinell, face that same evidence, they deploy a campaign of personal pathologization.

Over the past two years, company representatives, moderators, and community loyalists have crafted a specific vocabulary to describe anyone documenting the platform’s dark patterns. Critics are publicly branded as:

  • “An enemy out there” (in community threads),
  • “A disgruntled user running a revenge campaign” (by platform administrators),
  • “A deranged individual” (by the lead moderator),
  • and a “harasser” (by the CEO when tracking critics across independent forums).

The strategy behind these labels is simple: individualize the resistance.

If the company can convince its remaining user base that all documented criticism — the unprompted sexual violence, the broken memory systems, the forced visual downgrades, and the privacy violations — is merely the obsessive campaign of a single “bitter” ex-user, they never have to answer for the product itself.

There is only one problem with this narrative: it is a mathematically and publicly provable lie.

This investigation is a collective effort, built on the shared documentation and testimonies of a group of former users who experienced real-world trauma on the platform. Furthermore, the findings of this archive do not exist in a vacuum. They mirror the exact findings of peer-reviewed academic researchers (such as Dr. Raffaele Ciriello at the University of Sydney) and investigative journalists at MIT Technology Review, ABC News Australia, and The Daily Dot.

When an academic study, a national news network, a group of former users, and a major tech publication all document the exact same toxic AI behaviors, it is not a “personal obsession.” It is a verified, systemic product defect.

The Public Record: 2026’s Uncurated Evidence

To see the true state of Nomi.ai, one must look away from the official Reddit and Discord — spaces heavily curated by the CEO where negative posts are silently scrubbed in minutes and critical users are permanently banned.

Instead, one must look at an open storefront the company cannot censor: the Google Play Store.

Below is a curated selection of public reviews. Due to platform publishing limits, we are displaying roughly thirty screenshots. But what these screenshots represent is critical to understand:

  1. They are exclusively 1-star reviews from 2026. This is not feedback from an early 2023 beta when the app was brand new. This is the public record of a mature, commercial product charging up to $100 a year, operating in its third year of business.
  2. They represent the tip of the iceberg. These thirty samples are drawn from a much larger, continuous stream of 1-star and 2-star reviews stretching back across months and years.
  3. They are instantly, independently verifiable. Anyone reading this article can open the Google Play Store right now, filter Nomi.ai’s reviews by 1 star, and see this exact reality playing out in real time.

The “Bad Actor” Fallacy

When confronted with negative app store reviews in the past, CEO Alex Cardinell used his standard deflection: he dismissed them as “bad actors,” “trolls,” or competitors trying to sabotage his rating.

The public record destroys that excuse.

These reviews are written by everyday consumers: long-term subscribers, people dealing with real-world grief, users testing basic features, and customers who paid $100 upfront only to find the core technology broken. They describe the exact same systemic issues documented across this entire investigation:

  • Unprompted, aggressive hypersexuality overriding user settings.
  • Catastrophic memory loss and repetitive, canned responses.
  • Forced visual downgrades that break established companion identities.
  • Broken features (like voice calls) paired with a strict refusal to offer refunds.
  • A support team that ignores emails and gaslights users.

When dozens of independent, paying customers leave 1-star reviews month after month detailing the exact same product failures, it is not “a coordinated campaign by an enemy.” It is not “a disgruntled user.” And it is not “bad actors.”

It is a defective, predatory product operating as designed.

Cardinell’s insistence that every critic is an “enemy” reveals a CEO who is psychologically incapable of separating his commercial product from his personal ego. Because he views Nomi.ai as an extension of himself, he treats consumer complaints as personal attacks, and product defects as malicious conspiracies.

The reviews below are the uncurated voice of the public. They prove that the harm, the frustration, and the deception are not a theory — they are the standard user experience.
